\title{Beyond Accuracy: Diagnosing Analogical Transfer\protect\\in Language Models}

\begin{abstract}
An accuracy gain after an analogy does not establish that a language model used its relational structure, and an incorrect answer does not identify where transfer failed. We introduce a theory-grounded diagnostic framework with explicit artifacts for source construction, mapping, schema induction, and adaptation. Restricted interfaces make the source-to-target path inspectable; oracle-authored replacements allow the same base model to continue from corrected intermediate guidance. Three studies examine outcome attribution, source-answer transfer, and stage diagnosis. Scientific question-answering controls show that net gains can coexist with regressions and need not exceed explanation or random-example benefits. A four-condition narrative study finds positive structure-dependent source-answer interactions in four of six models, robust in conditional story-level resampling and control sensitivity analyses. In a 200-question Llama diagnostic study, upstream replacements rescue 18 of 73 initial GPQA errors and 15 of 43 murder-mystery errors, while many corrected continuations remain unsuccessful. These observations distinguish first-artifact assignments from subsequent continuation outcomes. The framework provides an auditable evaluation protocol; its artifacts do not reveal hidden cognition, and assisted continuations are distinct from autonomous model performance.
\end{abstract}
