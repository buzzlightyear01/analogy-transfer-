\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{RQ1: a net gain leaves attribution unresolved}
\label{sec:phase1results}
The concept-focused 300-word analogy does not exceed both random controls for any of the four solvers in the retained full-cohort results (Table~\ref{tab:phase1} in Appendix~\ref{app:execution}). Llama performs slightly better with a same-domain random source; Qwen matches that control and trails the other-domain control; Mistral trails both. Gemma lies between its random controls, with the additional composition caveat. This does not show that relation is irrelevant in every item; it shows that accuracy here does not establish a general benefit of the intended relation.

\paragraph{The baseline changes the interpretation.}
Mistral improves from 16.96\% with CoT to 27.01\% with the 300-word teacher analogy, but a 300-word non-analogical explanation reaches 27.68\%. A gain against CoT is therefore insufficient evidence that analogical presentation supplies the advantage. For Qwen, self-analogy reaches 43.30\%, compared with 42.19\% for CoT and 36.61\% for the teacher analogy. The conditions differ in the assistance and requested procedure; they are not interchangeable measures of one capacity.

\paragraph{Improvements coexist with regressions.}
Mistral's teacher analogy repairs 86 CoT errors and reverses 41 correct answers: 19.20\% and 9.15\% of the 448 shared questions. Their difference is a 45-question gain (10.04 percentage points). Two 300-word examples repair 105 answers and damage 44. Figure~\ref{fig:transitions} includes random controls, which also change both kinds of answer. These transitions are condition-associated outcomes; they do not establish a faulty analogy as the cause of each regression.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/phase1_transitions.pdf}
\caption{Mistral correctness transitions against CoT. Both directions use all 448 shared questions as denominator. Random controls can also improve or damage answers.}
\label{fig:transitions}

\paragraph{Mention does not resolve the ambiguity.}
Qwen refers to the 300-word example in 31.70\% of all responses. The rate is 31.10\% within correct answers and 32.04\% within incorrect ones. Mention can accompany misuse or rejection, while absent mention does not establish non-use. Similarly, increasing example count creates an opportunity for schema induction rather than direct evidence of it. These observations motivate evaluating correspondence and explicit intermediate content.

\subsection{RQ2: selective source-answer benefit is observable}
\label{sec:phase2results}
Table~\ref{tab:factorial} reports every cell and the interaction. Four models show positive interactions: Llama-8B, Llama-70B, Qwen-14B, and Qwen-32B. Their conditional story-resampling intervals remain above zero. The 1B and 3B models have zero mean interaction, despite different baseline accuracies and potentially useful corresponding source content. We interpret the pattern within the retained design, without a universal scaling or hidden-cognition claim.

The four-cell view matters. Llama-8B gains 13.59 points when a corresponding source answer is added, while the random-answer contrast is $-0.65$. Qwen-32B gains 5.34 points under correspondence and loses 7.28 under random assignment: its interaction is not wholly improvement from useful guidance. A positive interaction distinguishes conditions, not necessarily a uniformly beneficial intervention.

\paragraph{Control and extraction sensitivity.}
Removing the four same-story controls leaves 99 questions. Interactions remain positive at 13.80, 13.13, 9.60, and 12.63 points for Llama-8B, 70B, Qwen-14B, and 32B. Restricting to questions with valid outputs in every saved cell/run also leaves positive interactions: 14.05, 14.02, 12.07, and 10.00, on 102, 88, 58, and 65 questions. The substantial Qwen invalid-output counts make this check relevant. Its selected subsets cannot replace the primary results; Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity} reports all models and counts.

\paragraph{Answer positions provide a competing explanation.}
With a corresponding source answer, Llama-1B achieves 88.24\% when source and target answer positions match and 0.64\% when they differ, pooling three runs. This is consistent with predominant index following. Llama-8B and 70B retain 57.05\% and 65.38\% in the differing-position stratum, compared with 74.51\% and 79.08\% when positions match. The larger models' behavior is compatible with more than index copying, although position sensitivity persists. Different questions form the two strata, so this is supporting evidence rather than a controlled positional intervention.

Taken together, these results support selective benefit under an intended source relation in several evaluated models. They do not establish that every correct answer used a valid mapping, nor that the same model can construct and abstract adequate sources autonomously. The latter distinction motivates inspecting the staged path.

\subsection{RQ3: initial assignments and continuations differ}
\label{sec:phase3results}
\paragraph{Natural performance gives context.}
Table~\ref{tab:natural} compares direct and staged answers on matched 100-item sets. Llama's staged workflow answers 27 GPQA and 57 murder questions correctly. It improves over direct answering on GPQA but declines on murder mysteries. DeepSeek shows the same direction of change, with smaller murder decline. Multiple calls and retries are not matched to the direct baseline, so these results establish neither efficiency nor an accuracy advantage of the architecture.

\paragraph{An initial stage distribution is incomplete.}
The primary Llama cohort contains 73 initial GPQA failures and 43 murder failures. All GPQA failures receive initial R assignments in the retained diagnosis. Murder assignments are R in 19 cases, M in three, and A in 21. No initial S assignment appears in either cohort. Those labels describe the archived inspection order and criteria; their frequencies are not independently validated population estimates.

\centering

\paragraph{Upstream correction does not ensure completion.}
After the R replacement pass, 17 GPQA failures and 11 murder failures have successful continuations. After cumulative M replacement, the counts are 17 and 14; after S replacement, 18 and 15 (Figure~\ref{fig:repairs}). At that point 55 GPQA and 28 murder cases remain assigned to A: 75.34\% and 65.12\% of the original failure cohorts. The full state accounting appears in Appendix~\ref{app:states}.

The first GPQA assignments suggest attention to source construction. The corrected paths add a different observation: even with oracle-informed upstream artifacts, most initially unsuccessful Llama paths remain unsuccessful at adaptation. This is a conditional continuation outcome, not proof that adaptation was the hidden original cause. Conversely, the absence of initial S labels does not show that all schemas were adequate; S assignments appear after earlier corrections. A single initial label loses these distinctions.

The totals 45/100 GPQA and 72/100 murder combine natural successes with upstream-assisted successes. They must not be described as autonomous staged accuracy. Corrected final A outputs are excluded entirely. Because corrections accumulate, overlapping stage burdens also cannot be summed as numbers of distinct failed questions.

\paragraph{A recoverable schema case illustrates the distinction.}
In a saved DeepSeek murder case (item 87, question 0), the original schema has empty text, roles, relations, conditions, and inference rule, with \texttt{sufficiency=insufficient}. Adaptation reports that no schema is available, reasons from the target independently, and answers A while the benchmark key is B. A saved S-replacement continuation retains earlier stages, supplies an oracle-authored schema that distinguishes method-specific evidence from generic motive/access, and the same base model answers B. This example shows why an intermediate failure is more informative than either answer correctness or a verbal claim of analogy use. It is a documented assisted case, not an independent necessity test for S or a completed replication of the full cohort diagnosis.
