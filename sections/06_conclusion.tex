\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
We presented a theory-grounded framework that evaluates analogical reasoning through source retrieval, relational mapping, schema induction, and target adaptation. Explicit artifacts and information boundaries enable cumulative correction tests that separate a first failure from downstream difficulties. The experiments show why final accuracy and textual mention are insufficient, provide positive evidence for structure-dependent source-answer benefits in several models, and reveal adaptation problems that persist after upstream guidance is corrected.

The practical implication is to evaluate what an analogy changes, what makes its solution useful, and what prevents that solution from reaching the target. Better sources alone need not resolve transfer. Future evaluations can vary stage interfaces, expand source validation, and compare matched valid and malformed corrections to refine attribution. The framework provides a testable account of the observable source-to-target process while retaining the distinction between assisted continuation and independent model performance.
