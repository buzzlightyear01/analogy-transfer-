\section*{Limitations}
The framework diagnoses an imposed, text-based workflow. Its intermediates are behavioral artifacts, not direct observations of hidden model computation. Requiring fresh calls and restricting information may introduce difficulties absent from unconstrained solving. The four-stage synthesis is one operationalization of cognitive distinctions; alternative interfaces or orderings may yield different diagnoses.

The scientific control study controls particular alternative explanations but does not isolate every source of condition differences. Instructions differ between teacher-assisted and solver-generated conditions, and nominal word budgets do not guarantee equal token or computational budgets. Gemma's retained question counts vary across conditions. Overall differences for that model can therefore include changes in question composition; matched-item comparisons require shared identifiers. Recorded invalid answers and service failures can also influence accuracy. Llama's recovered scientific-control records combine an initial DeepInfra run with AvalAI recovery of unsuccessful requests.

The factorial transfer experiment uses 103 completed synthetic-source records rather than a representative sample of all MuSR questions. Human source validation is incomplete. Correspondence is an intended and partially reviewed construction property; random reassignment removes the designed correspondence but cannot guarantee the absence of incidental useful relations. The four same-story object-placement controls and task-specific length differences further limit isolation. Global narrative-length matching does not match each pair's information content. The experiment manipulates source-answer \emph{availability}, not correct versus deliberately incorrect source answers.

The model-size comparison also changes Llama generation, provider, and sometimes quantization. The 1B and 3B models use Cloudflare, 8B uses Novita FP8, and 70B uses DeepInfra; Qwen uses DeepInfra FP8. Consequently, these observations do not identify a pure parameter-count effect. Two or three repeated executions describe run variability rather than population uncertainty. Object-placement questions can share stories, and comparisons within answer-position strata do not control item difficulty. We make no significance claim or universal scaling claim from these results.

The diagnostic study covers one reported base model and 100 questions per dataset. Diagnosis focuses on unsuccessful staged outputs and does not estimate unconditional failure rates or spontaneous analogy use. Human review covers 30\% of questions and associated outputs, without a reported inter-annotator agreement estimate. GPT-6 Sol authors corrections with access to the reference answer. Even without explicit answer disclosure, answer-informed sources or schemas can carry strong solution guidance. Rescue is therefore an assisted behavioral outcome, conditional on the oracle-informed artifact and the preceding correction history. Final-answer replacements are not independent model successes. These limits motivate reporting natural performance, cumulative upstream rescue, and oracle answer agreement separately.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
We study existing scientific and synthetic narrative benchmarks and model-generated sources. Improvements in benchmark performance do not establish reliable scientific or educational advice. An analogy can be persuasive while omitting a decisive constraint; the observed answer regressions reinforce the need to check applicability before deploying analogy-based assistance. Dataset and model access remain subject to their respective licenses and service terms. Generative AI assisted conversion of the research manuscript into English prose and LaTeX; the authors remain responsible for checking the claims, citations, numerical results, and final text. The use of models to generate sources and reference-informed corrections is described in the method.
