\section{A Theory-Grounded Diagnostic Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
\subsection{Theory translated into information contracts}
Structure-mapping theory distinguishes relations between systems from resemblance between their attributes \citep{gentner1983structure}. Systematicity favors connected relations over isolated local matches \citep{clement1991systematicity}, while computational structure mapping makes consistency constraints explicit \citep{falkenhainer1989sme}. Access to a source is separable from the soundness of an inference drawn from it \citep{gentner1993roles}. Comparative encoding can support schema induction \citep{gick1983schema,gentner2003learning}, and applying that schema to new constraints remains a further challenge \citep{novick1991math}. These distinctions motivate separately inspectable outputs. They do not prescribe a unique linear architecture.

Let $x$ denote a target with choices, $r$ solved sources, $m$ their comparative mapping, $s$ a schema, and $a$ the target solution. Our permitted flow is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r&=R(x), & m&=M(r),\\
s&=S(m), & a&=A(x,s).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:flow}
\end{equation}
Each stage is a fresh call without hidden conversational history. Only the designated artifact passes forward. The target is available to $R$ and $A$, but not to $M$ or $S$. The reference answer is unavailable throughout the natural workflow.

\paragraph{Source construction (R).}
The historical stage name is \emph{retrieval}; operationally, the model generates three solved problems. We observe those sources, not retrieval from internal memory. A source can resemble the target topically yet omit the relation needed for a solution. An incorrect source solution can also contaminate every later artifact.

\paragraph{Comparative mapping (M).}
Mapping aligns the generated sources with one another, recording role correspondences, common relations, suitability, and important differences. It is not a direct source-to-target mapping because the target is withheld. The final connection to the target occurs in $A$. Connected relations and their constraints matter more than renamed entities \citep{gentner1997mapping}.

\paragraph{Schema induction (S).}
The model sees the mapping artifact rather than the raw narratives. Its output states a solution rule and when that rule is applicable. A slogan such as ``compare the evidence'' does not specify how evidence distinguishes alternatives; an over-specific rule may merely restate one source. The schema must preserve useful operations while identifying boundaries of generalization.

\paragraph{Adaptation (A).}
The model instantiates schema roles in the target, checks constraints, makes necessary adjustments, and answers. Correctness and schema use are separate: a solver may reject a schema and solve independently, or report using it while making an invalid inference. Output fields and self-reports are observable traces rather than proof of faithful reasoning.

As an illustrative theoretical example, the army/radiation analogy preserves a convergence relation: strong force along one route is unsafe, weak forces alone are insufficient, and coordinated weak forces combine at a goal \citep{gick1980analogical}. Source construction must supply that relation; mapping aligns its roles; a schema preserves its conditions; adaptation checks whether the target satisfies them. This illustration is not one of our benchmark observations.

The interfaces deliberately test a source-comparison-to-schema-to-target route. Withholding information makes the support for each call traceable, but may impose bottlenecks absent from ordinary prompting. We therefore evaluate behavior under these contracts rather than identifying a universal sequence of cognitive operations.

\subsection{Inspection and cumulative continuation}
\label{sec:repair}
On a naturally unsuccessful path, artifacts are inspected in stage order and the first inadequate output is assigned a stage label. This ordering distinguishes upstream deficiencies from later ones without making the label a unique causal diagnosis. A malformed artifact and a semantically inadequate artifact also require different explanations; valid JSON alone is insufficient.

We replace the assigned artifact, keep earlier artifacts, and rerun downstream stages with the same base model. A correct final answer counts as an \emph{oracle-assisted continuation success}. If the answer remains incorrect, the new path is inspected and a further reference replaces its first inadequate artifact, retaining earlier corrections. The protocol records an initial assignment and a trajectory of corrected continuations. A question may contribute to several stage burdens, so those burdens are not a partition of questions.

In the primary study, GPT-6 Sol authors reference artifacts with access to the target answer: \emph{open-oracle authoring}. Upstream references are intended to respect stage-content requirements and avoid explicit disclosure of the target answer. The continuing base model receives the artifact rather than the key. Nevertheless, a reference can indirectly carry solution guidance, and its quality is not guaranteed simply because a stronger model produced it.

These interventions measure response to a particular corrected input under an accumulated history. They do not isolate the effect of correcting a stage from changes in wording, information, or stochastic continuation. A subsequent deficiency is observed in a new continuation; we cannot prove it already existed in the unexecuted counterfactual continuation of the original path.

Replacing $A$ directly supplies the final answer and leaves no downstream base-model call. Such outputs are excluded from continuation-success counts. This distinction separates correct oracle answers from successful use of upstream guidance by the original solver.
