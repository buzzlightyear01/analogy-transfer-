\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\paragraph{Analogy and theory.}
ARN and AnaloBench evaluate identification of narrative correspondences \citep{sourati2024arn,ye2024analobench}. Recognizing an analogue does not establish adaptation of its solution. \citet{petersen2026modelling} connect cognitive theories of analogy with NLP, including multiple processes beyond mapping. We build on these distinctions rather than claiming a new cognitive decomposition. Our contribution is an operational source-comparison-to-schema-to-target route with recorded interfaces and cumulative reference-assisted continuations.

\paragraph{Exemplars and outcome attribution.}
Analogical prompting generates relevant exemplars before solving a target \citep{yasunaga2024analogical}; SCUA studies scientific explanations through teacher analogies \citep{yuan2024boosting}. \citet{qin2025relevant} show that random examples can perform comparably, making relevance-based interpretations uncertain. Our controls extend the descriptive analysis to paired improvements and regressions. The factorial source-answer experiment complements this concern with positive evidence of selective answer benefit. Its source corpus is answer-informed, and correspondence is designed rather than independently established for every item.

\paragraph{Intervening on reasoning traces.}
\citet{lanham2023faithfulness} test how answers respond to changes in CoT, distinguishing stated reasoning from model behavior. Our replacements similarly test behavioral response to explicit text, but intervene at theory-motivated analogy interfaces and preserve a cumulative correction record. The general idea of intervention is not new. The particular contracts specify what content is available for comparison, abstraction, and target application, while our accounting excludes oracle final answers from solver success.
